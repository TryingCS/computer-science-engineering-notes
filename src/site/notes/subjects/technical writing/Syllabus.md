### **Chapitre I : Expression & Communication**

This chapter focuses on the core methodologies of academic sharing, structuring scientific documents, presenting research, and professional integration.

#### **1. Introduction: The Scientific Communication Ecosystem**

- **Key Topics:**
    
    - **Why share research:** Peer validation, visibility/valorization for the researcher, contributing to the advancement of science, and building academic and industrial collaborations.
        
    - **Communication Channels:** Written (articles, theses, technical reports, posters), oral (conferences, seminars, defenses), and visual (schemas, figures, slides, infographics).
        
    - **Scientific Writing Specifics:** Rigorous criteria including objectivity, precision, clarity, logical organization, and proper citation.
        
- **📝 Exercise 1:** Choose an AI scientific article and analyze its structure (specifically identifying the IMRaD sections). Point out the strengths and weaknesses of how the research is communicated.
    

#### **2. Written Communications: The Research Thesis (Mémoire)**

- **Key Topics:**
    
    - **Writing Fundamentals:** Adopting an academic style (concise sentences, neutrality, technical vocabulary), utilizing the **IMRaD structure** (Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion), and performing rigorous proofreading using tools like Grammarly, Antidote, or LanguageTool.
        
    - **Thesis Structure:** Page de garde, Abstract, Introduction, Theoretical framework, Methodology, Results and discussion, Conclusion and perspectives, Bibliography, and Annexes.
        
    - **The Theoretical Framework (State of the Art):** Document research strategies (using databases like IEEE, Springer, Scopus), critical reading, and identifying research "gaps".
        
    - **The Methodology Framework:** Documenting data, software, Python libraries, platforms, and detailing the experimental protocol.
        
    - **Data Presentation & Interpretation:** Factual reporting of results with tables/figures, followed by deep discussion and comparison to the state of the art.
        
    - **Intro & Conclusion:** Structuring contexts, problem statements, and roadmaps for introductions, and summarizing contributions, limits, and future outlooks for conclusions.
        
    - **Bibliography:** Academic styles (IEEE, APA) and citation management using Zotero or Mendeley.
        
- **📝 Exercise 2:** Write a 15-line introduction for a fictional thesis titled _"L’application des réseaux de neurones à la classification d’images médicales"_.
    

#### **3. Oral Communications: Preparation and Delivery**

- **Key Topics:**
    
    - **Preparation:** Audience analysis, building a strong **scientific storytelling** narrative, and applying the **10/20/30 slide rule** (10 slides, 20 minutes, font size \(\ge\) 30).
        
    - **Delivery:** Verbal communication (pace, articulation, pauses), non-verbal cues (posture, gestures, eye contact), and managing stress.
        
    - **PFE Defense (Soutenance):** Standard 15–20 minute format, handling jury questions, and maintaining a balanced attitude of confidence, humility, and academic rigor.
        
- **📝 Exercise 3:** Prepare a 5-slide outline designed to present your Master's/Engineer's project.
    

#### **4. Professional Development**

- **Key Topics:** Designing an effective CV and cover letter, preparing for job interviews (including a 2-minute pitch and using the **STAR method**: Situation, Task, Action, Result), and formulating a career plan in the field of AI.
    
- **📝 Exercise 4:** Write a 2-minute elevator pitch to introduce yourself to an AI recruiter.

### **Chapitre II : LaTeX (Travaux pratiques)**

This chapter serves as the hands-on practical lab to master LaTeX for typesetting high-quality scientific documents.

- **Key Topics:**
    
    - **LaTeX Basics:** Setup and editors (TeXstudio, Overleaf), creating a basic document structure (`\documentclass`, `\begin{document}`, `\end{document}`), and text formatting (bold, italics, itemized lists).
        
    - **Long Document Structuring:** Managing sections and subsections, generating an automatic Table of Contents, and setting up cross-references (`\label`, `\ref`).
        
    - **Tables:** Using the `tabular` environment, handling column alignment, and configuring floating tables with captions.
        
    - **Images:** Inserting graphics (`\includegraphics`), using the `figure` environment, and controlling image positioning.
        
    - **Mathematical Equations:** Typesetting inline and block math, numbered equations, fractions, indices, exponents, and matrices.
        
    - **Bibliography with BibTeX:** Setting up a `.bib` file, referencing citations with `\cite`, and rendering the bibliography using `\bibliography`.
        
- **📝 Exercise 5:** Create a 2-page mini-document in LaTeX containing an introduction, a formatted table, a figure, a mathematical equation, and a bibliography.
