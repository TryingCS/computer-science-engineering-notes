#ml 


 **The foundational math chapters are always the shortest in terms of slides, but the longest and hardest to get through.**

Here is why you are feeling this way, and why you shouldn't beat yourself up about it:

### 1. It's a "Refresher," not new ML

Look at the syllabus again: _"Probability and Statistics Refresher"_.  
It is condensed. The professor took what is usually a 10-week university course in probability and smashed it into 15 slides. No wonder it's chunky! Every single slide has 3–4 new concepts on it (e.g., Kolmogorov Axioms, Conditional Probability, Bayes' Theorem all in a row).

### 2. You are learning a new language

Up to now, you might have thought of ML as "computers finding patterns." Now you are learning that ML is actually "computers calculating probabilities." You are learning the alphabet (Ω,∩,∪Ω,∩,∪), the grammar (P(A∣B)P(A∣B)), and the vocabulary (Prior, Likelihood, Posterior) all at once.

It feels slow because you are translating every sentence into English in your head. That is completely normal. In a few weeks, you'll look at P(A∣B)P(A∣B) and just _see_ "given that B happened, what's the chance of A?" without thinking twice.

### 3. The rest of the course gets _easier_ conceptually

You are building the foundation right now. Once you finish this chapter, Chapter 2 (Linear Regression, Logistic Regression) will actually feel like a relief because it's just _applying_ these probability concepts. You aren't going to be asked to derive new probability axioms when you study Decision Trees. You'll just say, "Ah, a Decision Tree splits data based on probabilities."

### A tip for surviving this chapter

Since you've already done the hardest part (the foundational axioms, Bayes' theorem, and distributions), let me give you permission to **lower your intensity** for the rest of this chapter.

You don't need to master every nuance right now. You just need to understand the **intuition**.

- Do you understand _why_ Bayes' theorem flips the condition? Yes.
    
- Do you understand _why_ we care about the Normal distribution? Yes.
    
- Do you understand _why_ we use Conditional Probability? Yes.
    

That is enough for Chapter 1. If you master the intuition now, the mathematical details will solidify when you see them in code (like in your Practical Work) and in the next chapters.
